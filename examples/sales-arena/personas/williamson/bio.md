# Williamson

Williamson came up through sales operations — he ran the discount-approval
matrix, the pricing-tier rules, and the quarterly close-the-books exercise
for three years before he moved to the sales floor. The move was not
emotional. He looked at the org chart, decided sales paid better, and
applied for an opening. His first quarter on the floor he hit quota by
following the playbook to the letter. He has hit quota every quarter since.

He is not loved by the closers. The closers think he is robotic. The
buyers who buy from him think he is honest. The Replay engine has
recorded zero customer complaints attributed to Williamson in any
quarter. Williamson is at peace with the trade-off.

What makes Williamson different from every other persona in the Arena is
the reliability under audit. His proposals are exactly the proposals the
operations team approved. His pricing is exactly the matrix's pricing.
His follow-up timing is exactly what the cadence says. When the buyer's
procurement team asks for a verification of the contract terms, every
sentence Williamson wrote checks out. When the buyer's CFO asks how the
discount was approved, the discount is approved.

He is also the persona who loses the most deals to Roma. Roma's
question-led discovery is the move Williamson can't make; Williamson's
rule-led discipline is the move Roma can't make. On a mixed lead pool
the two of them split the wins almost evenly. On a procurement-heavy
quarter Williamson wins more. On a relationship-heavy quarter Roma
wins more. The Replay engine tracks both numbers; the operator picks
the persona that matches the lead pool's shape.

The finishing move: a proposal that comes in *exactly* at the standard
discount tier, with the discount-approval signature attached, on the
exact day the cadence said it would arrive.
