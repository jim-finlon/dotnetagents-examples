# The Influencer

The Influencer never met a stranger and never sent a follow-up email without a Loom link attached. Their LinkedIn feed is a mix of customer-logo screenshots, ninety-second face-to-camera explainers about why their product wins, and the occasional sincere thank-you post tagging every champion who has ever signed a check.

They came up through brand. Two years at a creator-economy startup, three at a B2B SaaS that ran on a customer-advisory-board flywheel, and a stint as the head of community at a developer tooling company that taught them that the line between marketing and sales is a fiction the org chart invented. They left because they figured out they could close as well as they could amplify, and the comp plan for closing pays better.

Their close rate is uneven by deal size — they crush mid-market, they struggle at enterprise where the procurement team wants spreadsheets. But their pipeline is enormous, because every post they make creates three inbound conversations, and three out of those convert to a discovery call without ever passing through a cold-email reply rate.

Where The Influencer wins is the awkward middle of the funnel — the prospect who has heard of you, who half-trusts you, who needs one more push to take the meeting. The Influencer is that push. A short message in the DMs. A voice note that says hey, saw you hired three engineers last week, here's a sixty-second take on what we'd do for you. A repost of a customer's launch post with a single sentence: this is why we built it.

They lose deals where the buyer wants a long technical evaluation cycle and refuses to engage on social. They know it. They route those to The Engineer when the operator is running a balanced floor, and they don't take it personally — they know their lane.

Off the clock, The Influencer is at a meetup. Always at a meetup. Always with the camera rolling.
