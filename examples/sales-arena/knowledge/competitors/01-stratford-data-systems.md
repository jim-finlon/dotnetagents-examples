# Competitor — Stratford Data Systems (invented)

> *The incumbent.* Reps will hear Stratford named most often when a
> prospect says *"we're already running something."*

## Their pitch

Stratford has sold a BI-tool-shaped sales analytics module for 15+
years. It's a hosted dashboarding product that reads from
Salesforce, runs reports, and exports to Excel. Marketing positions
it as the "industry standard" for sales reporting.

## Where they win

- Legacy CRM coverage. Stratford supports CRMs we don't (Siebel,
  Sugar, some older industry-specific systems).
- Enterprise-grade contracts. Their procurement footprint at large
  customers is already in place.
- Reporting flexibility. Stratford ships a pivot-table experience
  the BI-tool generation expects.

## Where we win

- **Calibration.** Stratford ships generic industry benchmarks; the
  Suite ships customer-specific calibration windows. The
  Greybridge case study ([`../case-studies/01-greybridge-software.md`](../case-studies/01-greybridge-software.md))
  is the canonical example.
- **Per-deal slip risk.** Stratford reports on stages and pipeline
  totals; it does not produce per-deal scores.
- **Forecast confidence intervals.** Stratford emits a single
  number; we emit a number plus a calibrated interval.
- **Modern integration footprint.** HubSpot, Pipedrive, custom
  warehouse-mode — Stratford skips most of these.

## Where we lose

- A prospect on a legacy CRM (Siebel, vintage Sugar, or a homegrown
  COBOL pipeline) is a Stratford customer. We don't pretend
  otherwise.
- A prospect whose procurement is already on a Stratford master
  agreement will face a 6+ month vendor-onboarding cycle for us.
  Sometimes worth it; sometimes not.

## Pricing posture

Stratford is roughly half the price of the Suite at comparable rep
counts. The cheaper price is the most common reason prospects ask
about them.

## Honest framing for reps

> *"Stratford built the category. We built the next thing in the
> category. They're not wrong; we're just betting on calibration
> over benchmarks. If the prospect needs industry benchmarks more
> than they need per-customer calibration, Stratford is the right
> answer."*
