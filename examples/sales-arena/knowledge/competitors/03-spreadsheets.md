# Competitor — Spreadsheets (the budget alternative)

> *The most-common competitor at the smallest end of the market.*
> Half the prospects who say "we already have a tool" mean a
> spreadsheet a RevOps analyst maintains by hand.

## Their pitch

A Google Sheets or Excel file that pulls from the CRM and projects
forward. Free. Maintained by whoever has the patience. Customized
exactly to the operator's preference.

## Where they win

- **Free.** Real winning move at the smallest end.
- **Customizable.** Operator can change anything at any time.
- **No vendor risk.** The CRM is the only vendor in the chain.
- **No security review.** The spreadsheet inherits the CRM's
  security posture; no new vendor to add to the questionnaire pile.

## Where we win

- **Calibration math.** The spreadsheet's forecast is the analyst's
  judgment plus a multiplier per stage. No calibration window, no
  confidence interval. The Suite ships the methodology.
- **Persistence.** When the analyst leaves, the spreadsheet leaves
  too. Vaughn Publishing
  ([`../case-studies/05-vaughn-publishing.md`](../case-studies/05-vaughn-publishing.md))
  hired Cheryl Yi precisely because their previous analyst left
  and the spreadsheet collapsed.
- **Refresh cadence.** Spreadsheets refresh when the analyst opens
  them. The Suite refreshes hourly (engagement signals) and
  nightly (full engine pass).
- **Honest signal volume.** The Competitive Intelligence module
  flags signals the analyst would never have time to catch.

## Where we lose

- Founder-led teams that don't have a forecast accuracy problem
  yet. Spreadsheets are fine until they aren't.
- Prospects whose CFO doesn't read the forecast. The Suite is most
  valuable when the forecast is a real input to a real decision.
- Prospects whose deal count is too low for calibration math to
  outperform a human analyst's judgment. Below ~30 active deals at
  any one time, the math gets noisy.

## Pricing posture

Free, until the analyst's time isn't.

## Honest framing for reps

> *"Spreadsheets are the right answer until your forecast is
> actually being committed to the board. The conversation flips
> when the spreadsheet has been wrong twice in a row, the CFO has
> noticed, and the analyst is the person delivering the bad news.
> The Suite is the next-step purchase after the spreadsheet has
> earned a forecast-trust problem."*
