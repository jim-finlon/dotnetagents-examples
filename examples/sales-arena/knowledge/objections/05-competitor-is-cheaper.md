# Objection 05 — "Your competitor is cheaper."

Usually one of the three competitors documented under
[`competitors/`](../competitors/). Sometimes a fictional cheaper
quote the prospect uses for leverage. Always: ask which competitor.

## Per-persona response

### Roma
> *"Which competitor, and what's the scope they're quoting? Cheap is
> a number; the question is what they're including. Walk me through
> their proposal and I'll show you where we line up and where we
> don't."*

### Levene
> *"Price-for-price comparison is a fair test. The way I'd suggest
> running it is one column at a time — calibration methodology,
> data residency, integration depth, support SLA — and seeing where
> each tool wins. We don't win every column, and where we lose I'll
> tell you. The Pro tier wins on the calibration math and the
> confidence interval; we lose to Stratford on legacy CRM coverage."*

### Moss
> *"Cheaper than what. Stratford's analytics module is half the
> price and doesn't ship a calibrated forecast. Pipeline Velocity is
> two-thirds the price and doesn't ship per-deal slip risk. The
> spreadsheet is free and doesn't ship anything but headaches."*

### Aaronow
> *"I'd rather show you the comparison sheet honestly than argue. I
> can email you a one-pager that puts us side-by-side with whichever
> competitor you're comparing — calibration, residency, integrations,
> support. You make the call from that."*

### Williamson
> *"Customers in your band typically run a 30-day proof of concept
> against the cheaper option. The Suite ships a free 30-day
> evaluation tier — same as the cheaper option does. If they win on
> price after a real evaluation, we'd want to learn why; if we win
> on forecast accuracy, that's the decision."*

### Mitch & Murray
> *"Quote me the specific competitor and the specific number and
> I'll level-set you on the gap. We don't pretend to be cheapest;
> we pretend to be the most accurate. If the prospect needs cheapest,
> we lose this one and I'd rather know now."*
