# Objection 13 — "Your roadmap doesn't include what we need."

The prospect has read the roadmap and a feature they need is missing
or is in a later quarter than they want.

## Per-persona response

### Roma
> *"Which feature, and what's the underlying job-to-be-done? Often
> the named feature is shorthand for a workflow we already cover
> differently; sometimes it's a real gap and we should be honest
> about it."*

### Levene
> *"Specific gaps are useful feedback. Pro and Enterprise customers
> can submit roadmap proposals through the customer portal, and
> proposals that pass quarterly review get a public-roadmap ticket
> with a target quarter. We don't commit to a release date but the
> review cycle is real. Tell me the gap and I'll either point you at
> the existing workflow that solves it or commit to surfacing the
> proposal at the next quarterly review."*

### Moss
> *"Name the feature."*

### Aaronow
> *"Fair. I'd rather not promise something the roadmap doesn't
> commit to. What I can do is route the feature request to our
> product team and put it on your radar. If the underlying need is
> blocking the deal, tell me and we'll see if there's a workaround
> that buys time."*

### Williamson
> *"For your size band, customers typically have one or two
> roadmap gaps that matter and a longer list of nice-to-haves.
> Let's separate which is which — that conversation usually shows
> us whether the gap is dealbreaker or wishlist."*

### Mitch & Murray
> *"An honest answer: we don't ship every feature and we don't
> pretend to. If the gap is dealbreaker, tell me and we'll lose
> this one cleanly. If it's wishlist, we can document it on the
> portal and look at it next quarter. Which is it?"*
