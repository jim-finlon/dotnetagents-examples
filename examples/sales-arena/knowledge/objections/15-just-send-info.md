# Objection 15 — "Just send me info, I'll read it and get back to you."

The polite no. Sometimes legitimate; usually a stall. The job is to
distinguish without irritating the prospect.

## Per-persona response

### Roma
> *"Glad to send the materials. Before I do — what specifically
> would you want me to focus on, and what does 'getting back to me'
> look like? If you can name the next step, the materials I send
> are different."*

### Levene
> *"Absolutely. I'll send the product one-pager, the security pack,
> and the pricing tier sheet today. The honest concern I'd raise:
> a lot of materials get forwarded into an inbox and never
> resurface, so I'd rather offer to walk you through the
> calibration section live in 15 minutes than send 40 pages you
> won't read. Up to you which is more useful."*

### Moss
> *"What do you want me to send, and when do you call me back?"*

### Aaronow
> *"Sure. I'll have the materials in your inbox by end of day. If
> it'd help, I can also send a calendar hold for a 15-minute
> follow-up two weeks from now — easy to cancel if the timing
> doesn't work, hard to remember if it's not on the calendar."*

### Williamson
> *"For prospects in your size band, the materials that move the
> needle are the case-studies and the calibration-methodology
> one-pager. I'll send those today plus the pricing sheet. I'll
> follow up in two weeks unless you tell me to move faster or
> slower."*

### Mitch & Murray
> *"Honest reply: 'send me info' is sometimes the polite no, and
> if it is, I'd rather know now and stop bothering you. If it's a
> genuine read-and-decide step, tell me what you want me to send
> and I'll send exactly that. The dishonest play is dumping forty
> pages and pretending I expect you to read them."*
