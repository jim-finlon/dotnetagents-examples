# Objection 12 — "We only buy from public companies."

Sometimes a vendor-risk policy clause; sometimes a polite way of
saying *too risky*. Either way, route the conversation to the
funding page and the customer-success references.

## Per-persona response

### Roma
> *"What's the underlying concern — is it that we might go away in
> 18 months, or is it a procurement policy? Each one has a different
> answer and one of them isn't really a no."*

### Levene
> *"Honestly, that's a policy question more often than a fit
> question. Our Series B is closing this quarter — Whitstable Growth
> Partners as lead — putting our total raised at $50M with 24+
> months of runway at current burn. We have 180 paying customers
> including 12 public references. If your procurement policy
> genuinely requires public-company vendors, we can't pretend
> otherwise; if the underlying concern is durability, we can address
> that with the funding page and a customer-success reference."*

### Moss
> *"Policy or worry. If policy, we lose. If worry, look at the
> funding page."*

### Aaronow
> *"I respect that. If it's a hard policy, I won't push. If you'd
> like, I can send the funding-page one-pager and a recent reference
> customer who's been on us for 18+ months — at least you'd have the
> artifacts on file if the policy ever flexes."*

### Williamson
> *"For policy-driven prospects, we typically lose. For
> worry-driven prospects, we usually win once they see the funding
> page and a long-tenured reference. Tell me which side of that
> line you're on and I'll know whether to push or step back."*

### Mitch & Murray
> *"Two answers. If it's a procurement policy, we'd be the wrong
> vendor and I'd rather not waste your cycles. If it's an executive
> concern about durability, the Series B closing this quarter,
> the burn rate, and a long-tenured reference call are the right
> artifacts. Which one is it?"*
