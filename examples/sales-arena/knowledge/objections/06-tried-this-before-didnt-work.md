# Objection 06 — "We tried something like this before and it didn't work."

The "burned by analytics" objection. The prospect has bought a
pipeline-analytics tool before, and the tool died on the shelf because
nobody used it, the data was dirty, or the reports lied.

## Per-persona response

### Roma
> *"What didn't work — the data, the adoption, or the answer the
> tool gave? Each of those is a different conversation, and only one
> of them is a Suite question."*

### Levene
> *"That's the most common reason people churn out of this category,
> and it's the reason we built the calibration window into the
> product instead of shipping a generic industry benchmark. The Suite
> doesn't claim to know your data on day one. It says: give me 90
> days, here's what I think the forecast looks like, here's the
> confidence interval, calibrate me against your reality. That's a
> different deal than the tool that promised you 95% accuracy from
> the first connector handshake."*

### Moss
> *"What was the tool, what was the result, and what does the manager
> think went wrong?"*

### Aaronow
> *"Fair concern. The Starter tier is month-to-month for a reason —
> if the Suite doesn't move your forecast confidence in the first
> quarter, you cancel without a long-term commitment. We don't ask
> for an annual on Starter."*

### Williamson
> *"Most of our customers who say this had a tool that scored *reps*
> rather than *deals*. The Suite explicitly does not score reps.
> Rep-level scoring is what kills adoption — reps disengage from a
> tool that grades them. Deal-level scoring shows the manager which
> deal to ask about, not which rep to write up."*

### Mitch & Murray
> *"What I'd suggest is a structured pilot: pick the 20 highest-value
> open deals, drop them into the analytics engine, see what scores
> the engine assigns versus what the rep rollup says. Two-week
> exercise. If the gap is non-trivial, the Suite earns the next
> conversation. If the gap is noise, we shake hands and move on."*
