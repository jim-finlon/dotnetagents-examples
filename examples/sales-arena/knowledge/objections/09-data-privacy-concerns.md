# Objection 09 — "What about data privacy / GDPR?"

Distinct from generic security. The prospect is worried about what
the Suite reads, where it stores it, and what happens on a
data-subject-rights request.

## Per-persona response

### Roma
> *"Help me understand whether the concern is what we read, where we
> store it, or how we delete it on request — they're three different
> answers and the wrong one wastes both of our time."*

### Levene
> *"Three answers up front. We read CRM data, activity logs, and
> email metadata by default — never message bodies unless you opt in
> with a separate consent flow. We store EU customer data in
> Frankfurt and Dublin, no data leaves the EU. Right-to-erasure
> requests complete within 30 days with cryptographic key erasure;
> the audit log retains the deletion event for the legal minimum.
> The DPA documents all three."*

### Moss
> *"Metadata only by default. EU data stays EU. Deletion in 30 days."*

### Aaronow
> *"Standard concern. Our DPA is available on request; I can have it
> in your inbox within the hour with our DPO's signature. If your
> legal team needs custom DPA terms, we negotiate those on
> Enterprise tier."*

### Williamson
> *"Customers in your size band who care about GDPR typically run an
> EU-tenant deployment from day one. The Suite ships a documented
> EU residency contract; data does not cross to a US region. The
> custom-residency option on Enterprise tier covers more unusual
> jurisdictional requirements."*

### Mitch & Murray
> *"At an exec level the question is whether the Suite makes you a
> covered processor under your customers' DPAs. Answer: we sign as
> a processor with documented sub-processors, EU residency contract,
> and a 30-day breach-notification SLA. Your legal team can have the
> full DPA before the next call."*
