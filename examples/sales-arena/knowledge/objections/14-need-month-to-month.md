# Objection 14 — "We need month-to-month, not annual."

Usually a budget-flexibility ask, sometimes a commitment-aversion
signal. The Starter tier is already month-to-month; the question
mostly comes up for prospects pricing into Pro tier.

## Per-persona response

### Roma
> *"What's driving the month-to-month preference — uncertainty about
> fit, budget flexibility, or org volatility? Each one has a
> different answer."*

### Levene
> *"The Starter tier is month-to-month, no annual required. The Pro
> tier is quarterly commit with a 5% prepaid discount; we can ship
> a Pro tier on month-to-month at standard pricing without the
> discount. The economic reason behind the annual is that the
> calibration window pays off across multiple quarters; on
> month-to-month a customer can cancel before the calibration
> matures, which is fine for both of us — we'd rather not have you
> on a tier that doesn't earn its keep."*

### Moss
> *"Starter is month-to-month. Pro is quarterly. Pick a fit and
> we'll size it."*

### Aaronow
> *"Yeah, we can do month-to-month on Pro at standard pricing. Lose
> the prepaid discount, keep the flexibility. Does that solve the
> ask?"*

### Williamson
> *"For customers running an initial pilot, Starter on month-to-month
> is the typical entry. Once the calibration window completes (90
> days), customers who want the Pro features typically move to
> quarterly with the prepaid discount. We don't gate features behind
> the annual commit; the only thing the annual changes is price."*

### Mitch & Murray
> *"Two answers. We can ship Pro on month-to-month at the standard
> price; we lose the discount and you keep the option. Or you can
> start on Starter month-to-month, prove the calibration math
> against your data, and move up. Either is fine. The annual is a
> discount, not a contract obligation."*
