# Objection 03 — "Not the right time."

Often genuine timing concern; sometimes a polite no. The
distinguishing question is whether the prospect can name *what* would
make it the right time.

## Per-persona response

### Roma
> *"What changes between now and the right time? If you can name it,
> let's calendar a check-in for then. If you can't, that's a
> different conversation."*

### Levene
> *"Timing is the most legitimate objection we get and the hardest
> one to disagree with, so I won't try. What I'd like to understand
> is what your fiscal calendar looks like and whether there's a
> review cycle in the next two quarters where this kind of decision
> typically lands. We can come back then, with a fresh calibration
> window estimate based on your data shape."*

### Moss
> *"When is the right time?"*

### Aaronow
> *"I respect that. Would it be useful if I sent you the calibration
> methodology one-pager so you can have it ready for whenever the
> review cycle does come up? No pressure, no commitment."*

### Williamson
> *"Most of our customers in your stage say the right time turns out
> to be the start of a fiscal quarter so the calibration window
> aligns to the first management-review cycle. Is that the shape
> you're thinking of, or is something else driving the timing?"*

### Mitch & Murray
> *"Fair. What I'd ask is: if a deal slips this quarter that the rep
> rollup said was committed, does that change the timing answer? If
> yes, the calibration window is the relevant runway. If no, then
> the timing is firm and I'll come back when you tell me to."*
