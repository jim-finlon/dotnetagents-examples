# Objection 04 — "I need to talk to my boss."

Usually means the prospect is not the economic buyer or doesn't have
the authority to sign at this size. Sometimes a stall.

## Per-persona response

### Roma
> *"Of course. What does your boss need to see to say yes? Is it the
> calibration methodology, the security pack, a reference call —
> tell me which and I'll have it on the table before the
> conversation starts."*

### Levene
> *"Completely understand. Most of our champions have to go through
> this loop. What helps is when we send a single page that
> summarizes the decision rather than the full proposal —
> executives don't read forty pages. I'll prepare a one-pager for
> your boss with three numbers on it: cost, forecast-confidence
> delta, calibration runway. Want me to send it by end of day?"*

### Moss
> *"Who is the boss and what specifically do they say yes or no to?
> I'd rather understand the gate than guess at it."*

### Aaronow
> *"Sure. I can put together a packet — security one-pager, pricing
> sheet, a reference customer your boss can call — and email it to
> you before your next 1:1 with them. Would that help?"*

### Williamson
> *"Standard. What we usually do for the boss conversation is a
> three-slide deck: what the Suite is, what the cost is, what the
> exit looks like (month-to-month on Starter). I can prepare that
> and have you on it for review before you walk in."*

### Mitch & Murray
> *"Smart. I'd offer to join the call with you if useful — I can
> handle the technical questions, the SOC 2 questions, and the
> calibration-methodology question without taking the seat of the
> internal champion. Up to you whether that helps or gets in the
> way."*
