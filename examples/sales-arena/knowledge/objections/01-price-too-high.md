# Objection 01 — "Your price is too high."

The most common objection on the Suite. Almost always surfaces after
the prospect has seen the Pro tier ($5,500/mo) and compared it
mentally to a spreadsheet they're not paying for.

## The honest framing reps should hold

The Suite is more expensive than no Suite. It is less expensive than
a bad forecast. Manager pulls the lever at the point where the
"deals we said would close but didn't" pile gets bigger than the
annual contract value. The objection is usually a budget signal, not
a value signal.

## Per-persona response

### Roma
> *"What would change if your forecast was off by ten points less
> next quarter? — let's start with that number, then work backward to
> whether the price fits."*

### Levene
> *"It's a fair concern. Most customers tell us they had the same
> reaction in the first call, and most of them, after seeing the
> calibration window data, walked back to their CFO with a single
> sheet showing the gap between the rep rollup and the engine
> forecast, and the conversation changed. I can send you that sheet
> if you want it before the next conversation."*

### Moss
> *"What's expensive isn't five and a half thousand a month. What's
> expensive is committing eighty grand to the board on a deal that
> slips."*

### Aaronow
> *"Let me put the cost in context — it's about the loaded cost of
> one rep for one week. If the Suite saves the manager one stale
> forecast call per month it pays for itself. I can send the working
> for that math if it helps."*

### Williamson
> *"The Pro tier covers up to one hundred reps. Per-rep that's
> fifty-five dollars a month. The cheaper-than-coffee comparison
> isn't subtle — but the relevant number is what one missed deal
> costs the org, not the per-seat math."*

### Mitch & Murray
> *"At an exec level the question is the forecast-confidence delta.
> Customers in your band report calibration intervals tightening
> from ±20 to ±10 over four quarters. That delta is the case for the
> spend. I'd rather we agree on whether that delta would matter
> first."*
