# Case Study — Northwood Energy Services

> **Industry.** Energy-services + renewables consulting ·
> **Headcount.** ~520 · **Reps.** 71 · **CRM.** Salesforce ·
> **Tier.** Enterprise

## The before

Northwood is a hybrid sell — half SaaS subscription (analytics
licenses to utilities) and half consulting engagement (the team
does on-site assessments). Their CRO, **Anneliese Bremer**, had
been quoted three different forecast numbers in one week by three
different regional VPs.

## What changed

Northwood took the Enterprise tier with the operator-configurable
cohort comparison. The cohorts the Suite computed (by region and by
sell-motion type — SaaS vs consulting) showed Anneliese that the
disagreement was structural: each regional VP was right about their
own cohort and wrong about the others. The Suite's region-cohort
view became the standard executive forecast pack.

## The measured outcome

Forecast interval moved from `±20%` to `±11%` over four quarters.
Anneliese cites the case study at industry conferences. Northwood
is the second-largest Suite customer by ACV.

## Quotable

> *"Everyone was right about their own piece and wrong about the
> whole. The Suite let us see both."* — Anneliese Bremer, CRO,
> Northwood Energy Services

## Why this story matters

The multi-sell-motion case study. Reps working a prospect that sells
both subscription and services (the hybrid pattern is common in
managed-services, security, and energy) should reach for Northwood.
The cohort-comparison view is the feature that wins the deal.

## Honest caveat

Northwood's services-side forecast accuracy is *worse* than their
SaaS-side — same root cause as Fenwick. Reps should not pretend
the Suite is equally good at project-based revenue and subscription
revenue.
