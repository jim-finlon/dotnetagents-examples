# Case Study — Hartfield Industries

> **Industry.** Mid-market manufacturing-software · **Headcount.**
> ~340 · **Reps.** 41 · **CRM.** Salesforce · **Tier.** Pro

## The before

Hartfield bought the Suite on a Pro-tier quarterly commit after a
five-quarter forecast accuracy issue. Their COO, **Sigrun Petursson**,
wanted to use the Suite for both forecasting and rep coaching — the
Suite team pushed back: the Suite ships coaching prompts *for
managers*, not coaching surfaces *for reps*.

## What changed

Hartfield agreed to use the Suite the way it shipped. They ran the
manager-facing coaching-prompt loop for four quarters. The
prompt-promotion candidates the Training Agent surfaced were reviewed
weekly by Sigrun's regional managers.

## The measured outcome

Forecast confidence interval moved from `±19%` to `±14%` over four
quarters. The Suite team is the first to admit this is a smaller
delta than Stratham Logistics or Greybridge — Hartfield's CRM
activity logging was inconsistent (a known issue Sigrun called out
in the kickoff). The Suite cannot predict deals that aren't in the
CRM, and the case-study delta reflects that.

## Quotable

> *"The Suite told us our data was noisier than we admitted. That
> was the lesson worth $66,000 a year."* — Sigrun Petursson, COO,
> Hartfield Industries

## Why this story matters

The honest case study. Not every customer moves their forecast from
`±20%` to `±9%`; some only move to `±14%`. Reps should use Hartfield
when a prospect's CRM hygiene is visibly weak — the conversation is
about whether the customer is willing to fix the logging discipline
the Suite will expose. The delta is real but smaller, and the
Suite's value is partly the act of surfacing the data-quality
problem.
