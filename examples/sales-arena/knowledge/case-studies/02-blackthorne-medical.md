# Case Study — Blackthorne Medical Devices

> **Industry.** Medical-device sales (non-clinical) · **Headcount.**
> ~190 · **Reps.** 28 · **CRM.** HubSpot · **Tier.** Pro

## The before

Blackthorne sells imaging systems to hospital procurement teams. The
deal cycle is 9-14 months; the close rate is 12%. Misforecasts cost
the operations team — wrong forecast meant either a production hole
or a stalled commitment. Their VP Sales, **Tomasz Petrosian**, had
quarterly conversations with manufacturing that he dreaded.

## What changed

The Suite's calibration window (45 days on Pro, courtesy of the
cross-customer prior) showed that Blackthorne's deal-cycle median
was the dominant signal — deals that hit 240 days without a buyer-side
meeting were essentially never going to close. The pipeline view's
*"deals where rep says committed, engine says <50%"* list became
Tomasz's weekly conversation with the manufacturing planner.

## The measured outcome

Manufacturing-planning ratio of "ordered against committed deals" /
"ordered against deals that actually closed" moved from 0.71 to 0.93
over three quarters. Inventory holding cost down. Tomasz no longer
dreads the quarterly conversation.

## Quotable

> *"We didn't sell more, we sold less wrong."* — Tomasz Petrosian,
> VP Sales, Blackthorne Medical

## Why this story matters

Long-cycle deal businesses get an asymmetric lift from the Suite —
the engine's slip-risk score catches the kind of mid-cycle stall
that a rep sees as "still working it" and the manufacturing planner
sees as a stockout. Reps working long-cycle prospects (medical
devices, capital equipment, public-sector) should reach for this
case study.
